# End of flight: slide text and speaker notes

## What we simulated

We continued selected states from the broad 00:11 trajectory ensemble, carrying their position, speed, altitude and fuel uncertainty forward. Fuel exhaustion was conditioned to occur between 00:15 and 00:19 UTC. After exhaustion, we simulated six control families under each of two separate aerodynamic models, including steady and changing lift/bank commands. The calculation records impact time, location and velocity and can then apply selected final satellite observations.

## How long could flight continue?

In the models tested, the longest computed flights after fuel exhaustion lasted about 31.5 minutes with the lower-drag model and 26.7 minutes with the higher-drag model. These durations came from integrating the forces and motion to sea level; they were not imposed as cutoffs. The simulation allowed 60 minutes. Both longest examples began at 43,000 ft. Thus “roughly half an hour in the models tested” is justified; “30 minutes is the aircraft’s absolute limit” is not.

## Reading the time charts

The count charts show how many computed trajectories terminate in each one-minute bin, with each control and drag model kept separate. Counts reflect the sampling design and are not a probability density. The weighted charts also apply the R600 BTO and BFO at 00:19:29, using the ordinary 4,600 microsecond BTO bias correction and no additional BFO startup offset. R1200 is omitted here. Each curve is normalized over computed impacts within that model; the remaining model-domain outcomes are unresolved.

## What determines the result?

Starting height, speed and mass provide the available energy; lift, drag and the chosen manoeuvres determine how quickly it is lost. The simulation carries the changing state through ERA5 weather. Its longest sampled duration is not a mathematical optimization over all possible controls. The maximum-lift/drag controller favours glide range and does not by itself maximize time aloft. The latest computed UTC impact across all twelve families is 00:50:04; UTC impact time and minutes after exhaustion are different quantities.

## Limits relevant to the meeting

The exhaustion interval, control priors and public clean-flight aerodynamic approximations are conditional assumptions. Stall, breakup, body attitude, electrical restart and acoustic coupling are not resolved by these simulations. Model-domain exits are unknown outcomes, not impossible trajectories. The weighted curves remain provisional and have not established a stable, calibrated full posterior. Ocean drift, antenna gain, hydroacoustic association and seabed search non-detection are not applied in these time charts. An acoustic hypothesis must match location and time jointly; lying inside this overall time window alone is insufficient.
