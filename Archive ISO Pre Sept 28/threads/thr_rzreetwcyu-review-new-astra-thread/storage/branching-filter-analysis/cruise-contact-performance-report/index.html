Aircraft performance: a contact-state diagnostic

102 saved illustrative histories, 13,312 contact/mass/polar cases; no new trajectories or changed probability weights. Generated 2026-09-09T18:54:12.748670+00:00.

The cruise estimator currently has kinematic Mach, altitude, bank and rate limits. This check asks what steady lift and thrust the saved contact states would require, using each route’s fuel-compatible mass range and its original weather. It compares two separate public drag polars with the same nominal public thrust proxy. These are conditional models, not a calibrated performance deck for 9M-MRO.

The left chart shows the largest required lift coefficient among checked contacts for each example. Both polars use the same wing area, so this value is shared. Values above one expose a discrepancy under that particular comparison; they do not by themselves prove that the aircraft could not fly the route.

The discrepancy depends on the polar, including for steady flight. Simply applying either proxy as a hard rejection rule could create a model-dependent narrowing of the PDF. This audit supports carrying aircraft-performance assumptions explicitly and checking a suitable thrust/drag/fuel combination before such exclusions are introduced.

Scope of the calculation

Each positive flow-quadrature node retains the low and high compatible anchor-fuel quantities and propagates them through the saved mass maps. These endpoint cases are not random fuel samples or confidence bounds. The calculation maintains the recorded bank and vertical speed with zero speed and path-angle acceleration. It does not inspect manoeuvres between contacts, stall/buffet margins or transient control authority.

  • These 102 selected histories are illustrative; their counts cannot be interpreted as percentages of the full posterior.
  • Only saved contacts at or after the fuel anchor are checked; manoeuvres between contacts and before the anchor are untested.
  • Speed and flight-path-angle acceleration are zero in this diagnostic, even when the original process is changing them.
  • Every positive finite flow-quadrature node uses both compatible initial-fuel interval endpoints; these endpoint cases are not posterior mass samples.
  • Nominal OpenAP thrust is a conditional comparison, not a validated Trent 892B limit. No hard exclusions are applied.
  • conditional simplified two-shaft turbofan proxy; not a calibrated 9M-MRO Trent 892B-17 deck
  • uses pressure altitude with ISA temperature and OpenAP's zero-rate cruise equation
  • caller-declared multiplier and static cap are analyst choices, not source uncertainties
  • no bleed, installation, deterioration, throttle/spool, asymmetric-control, or windmilling model
  • audited B772 trajectory coverage is contextual and does not validate the thrust equation
  • the numerical proxy domain is broader than the audited B772 trajectory coverage
  • OpenAP B772 attached-flow polar; not calibrated B777-200ER or Trent 892 data
  • caller-declared thrust range is a conditional proxy, not an inferred engine envelope
  • midpoint constant-rate inversion requires subdivision across material state changes
  • numerical lift-coefficient bounds are not a validated stall or high-angle-of-attack model
  • heading dynamics, control-law authority, buffet, windmilling, and structural limits are absent
