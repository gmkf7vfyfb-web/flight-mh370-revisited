What the 00:19 measurements change

The current mixed impact maps use R600 only: its recorded 182 Hz BFO, no extra warm-up offset, and its BTO after the ordinary 4,600 μs channel correction. R1200 contributes neither BFO nor BTO to those maps. The comparisons below change this selection on exactly the same saved trajectories.

The saved fuel condition is 00:15:00–00:19:00 UTC, centred on 00:17:00. It has not been relabelled as a 00:17:30-centred condition. This is an assumed fuel-exhaustion interval, not a measured time or a uniform impact-time distribution. A changed fuel interval needs its own explicitly configured comparison.

Selected-run diagnostics

The table reports normalized probability over computed impacts. “Unknown final likelihood” is incoming fuel-conditioned mass whose selected radio prediction could not be computed. “Scored mass with impact” is a different quantity: some trajectories have computable radio predictions but later leave the aerodynamic model's domain. Those uncomputed impacts are omitted from the map, with their mass disclosed here.

Effective weighted rows describe weight concentration; they are not a count of independent posterior trajectories. Repeated 00:11 ancestors are tracked separately. A concentrated both-BFO case needs additional targeted numerical work before its contour can be treated as stable.

Follow-through: did refreshed numerical guidance help?

Two smaller independent runs used guide centres learned from the actual controls in the larger completed sample. Physics, fuel and probability corrections were unchanged. Both guide comparisons below use 2,048 draws per each of six cruise sources. These runs remain separate from the fourfold maps above; no new guide is silently made the default.

A smaller gap in one case does not establish general improvement or validate the underlying cruise/fuel model. The effective-row counts report weight concentration among computed impacts.

Why the numerical guide needs further work

The current joint guide steers fuel quantity and the first lift setting. Saved trajectories also contain important solutions with later lift changes before the final transmissions. The table diagnoses where the local guide contributes no sampling density: those locations are covered only by its broad uniform component. This is a property of the sampling method, not evidence against those trajectories and not the fraction drawn from a particular mixture component.

All entries condition on computed impacts, before drift and search evidence. Fuel-to-R600 is the elapsed time between each sampled fuel exhaustion and the first final transmission. These marginal summaries are not new control priors or causal sensitivity estimates. Runs and aerodynamic models remain separate.

Ordinary satellite/aircraft frequency compensation remains in the forward prediction; “no offset” means no added OCXO warm-up adjustment.

Both BFOs are predicted along one continuous trajectory, 8.027 seconds apart, sharing the steady-frequency-bias uncertainty carried from 00:11. That bias is marginalized jointly, not independently refitted at each burst. We do not assign probabilities to the competing validity scenarios or average incompatible aerodynamic models together.

The relaxed cruise source starts with 1.02 million candidates and allows 16 turn, 8 Mach and 8 altitude command starts. Actual sampled altitude, speed and mass at 00:11 enter the continuation. The 7th arc drawn on these plots is a FL400 reference; impacts are not forced onto it. The current mixed calculations use two separate positive-lift, three-target terminal control models. The older twelve-family report includes more permissive terminal alternatives.

Lift coefficient targets range from 0.05 to 1.2; bank targets from −60° to +60°, with finite lift-change and roll rates. Two public attached-flow drag polars are treated as alternatives. The integration allows up to 60 minutes after exhaustion; a 30-minute cap is not imposed. These broad terminal controls and public polars are not a calibrated model of every possible stall, breakup or pilot action.

The fuel calculation reuses the cruise fuel condition and replaces its projected post-00:11 burn with the actual powered continuation. It does not multiply the same fuel evidence twice. SATCOM scoring is conditional on a transmission being available: the current queue does not require a simulated APU/electrical restart to produce it. Acoustic energy coupling and body attitude are not inferred by these maps.

Drift, antenna gain, acoustic, Pléiades and searched-area likelihoods are omitted from these particular maps to isolate final-contact sensitivity. Search outlines are context only. The broader debris/search report continues separately. Fuel-conditioned synthetic cruise recovery remains unsuccessful; these are provisional conditional PDFs, not a validated final location estimate.
