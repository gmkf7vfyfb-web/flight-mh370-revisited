# Satellites and MH370: what has been said, done and claimed — literature and source review

Prepared 20 Oct 2026 for the MH370 Bayesian re-analysis (Journal of Navigation paper). Companion register: [satellite-literature-register.csv]({{artifact:1c65603d-df87-4d5e-b9da-e5a4c3f46fae}}) (91 rows; IDs O = official, S = states/operators/news, I = independent, A = academic/policy, R = retention). Earlier evidence note on the 2014 sightings: [research-satellite-imagery-2014.md]({{artifact:837df6c5-e49b-44a3-8ade-44591f8082ea}}). Grades: **A** official or peer-reviewed, read with page/section; **B** official or peer-reviewed, cited or abstract only; **C** news, community or unofficial, used as argument and never as data. In the register, each source is coded for (a) imaging of MH370 in flight, (b) observation of impact or aftermath, (c) debris sightings, (d) prior coincidence analyses: *positive* (reports a detection), *negative* (reports a search that found nothing), *claim* (unverified or withdrawn assertion), *silent*.

## Scope limits (read first)

- ATSB 2017, the Malaysian SIR 2018 (all 495 PDF pages), the 2015 Factual Information, the ATSB search-area report (rev. 30 Jul 2015), the 2014 Flight Path Analysis Update, the 2017 debris update, CSIRO Parts III-IV and the ICAO/APANPIRG WP30 paper were text-searched from the project Drive copies. The SIR appendices (50 files) were listed but not searched. The June/August 2014 editions of *Definition of Underwater Search Areas* and the Dec 2016 *First Principles Review* were not retrieved (ATSB website does not answer from the sandbox; not on Drive).
- Australian Senate Estimates Hansard was **not** searched through ParlInfo; only web search was used, which found no question on satellite imagery or military satellites (O28). No FOI release on satellite imagery was found (O29). Both are provisional absences.
- PPRuNe, the Independent Group statements, Duncan Steel's full archive and social media were not crawled. The statement that no prior coincidence analysis exists rests on web search plus the sources above and is **provisional**.
- AMSA, JACC, MOT, DIGO, GISTDA and Japanese items were taken from the project note of 17 Oct 2026 (read then, not re-read today).
- Academic SAR papers were read at abstract level only (grade B).

## 1. Bottom line

1. **Has satellite imaging of MH370 in flight been looked for?** Yes, but only by image search, never by orbital coincidence. ATSB states that imagery over the Malay Peninsula, the Strait of Malacca and later the Indian Ocean, for the flight time and some hours after, was examined, and that the aircraft was not identified in any of it (O01, PDF p.125/printed p.116). NASA examined all polar-orbiter imagery 19:00 UTC 7 Mar to 05:00 UTC 8 Mar for contrails and found one doubtful Terra MODIS candidate (O04). No sensor, scene or footprint list was published. A June 2014 NYT report says investigators had found no satellite image of the aircraft in flight (S18, grade C).
2. **Impact or aftermath?** The only official-adjacent statements are anonymous US officials (12 Mar 2014) that infrared early-warning satellites saw no midair explosion at loss of contact or in the hours immediately after it (S01-S03). These predate the southern-corridor finding (15 Mar); none addresses the 00:19 end of flight explicitly. One community plume claim (370location, 2016) was withdrawn by its author (I09). The Malaysian SIR is silent (O18).
3. **Debris?** Satellite imagery was requested from about 15 Mar (O02) and produced the known 16-27 Mar sightings (DigitalGlobe, Gaofen-1, Airbus, French Pléiades and radar, THEOS, Japanese IGS; O03, O05-O08, O14, O21-O24, S04-S15). None was relocated or linked to MH370 (O03, O25). The northern detections at 34-35.5 S were never searched by air (O03), and were re-analysed only in 2017 when the French MoD released native Pléiades images (O07, O14, O15). The COSMO-SkyMed contacts of 21 Mar are known only from an unnamed source (I01, grade C).
4. **Has anyone computed which imaging satellites had the aircraft or the impact area in their field of regard (the parent's analysis)?** Not found. Every source in the register is *silent* on (d). The closest items are the parent project's own nominal-timing check of the THEOS and DigitalGlobe images ({{artifact:837df6c5-e49b-44a3-8ade-44591f8082ea}}) and generic statements that satellites were tasked "near the 7th arc" (O17). The parent's coincidence tables appear to be new; the claim of novelty is provisional (see scope limits).
5. **Would the data still be held?** For civil and commercial SAR and optical systems, operators document long-lived archives (Maxar to 1999, Pléiades 2012-, COSMO-SkyMed 2008-, RADARSAT-2 14-year archive, TerraSAR-X raw data in the DLR archive, NRSC global data for specific studies; R01-R12). An archive holds a scene only if the satellite was tasked to acquire it; nothing in the literature indicates that any civil radar was tasked over the southern Indian Ocean before about 15-16 Mar 2014 (O02, S04, S18). For military systems (Lacrosse, Kosmos 2487, Yaogan, IGS) nothing is published (R15).

## 2. Findings by topic

**(a) In flight.** Official: O01 (negative, no sensor list), O04 (NASA contrail search, negative). News: S01-S03 (US infrared, negative), S07 (Tomnod imagery dated 9 Mar over the Gulf of Thailand, then the west of the peninsula and Andaman/Nicobar from 15 Mar; negative), S18 (negative). Community claims: Meteosat-7 and Electro-L contrails (I06, I07, I10), critiqued (I08) and judged by NASA as cloud features (O04); the 2014 "satellite video" (I11), traced to stock cloud photographs and a VFX asset. Academic: spaceborne SAR detection of airborne aircraft is a 2024-2026 research topic (A01 states "for the first time"; A02 Sentinel-1 time series; A03 Gaofen-3 open-sea aircraft imaging). Detection of parked aircraft in 10 m Sentinel-1 data needs change detection (A04). A 2014 SAR scene of a 777 at cruise would therefore have been an unrecognised, defocused and displaced return; no 2014 process would have flagged it. Daylight optical sensors can show airborne aircraft through band-to-band offsets (A05), but the flight after 18:22 was at night until near the end.

**(b) Impact/aftermath.** US infrared: S01-S03. MODIS plume candidates: I09 (withdrawn), O04 (cloud edge). SAR slick detection is well established in general (A08) but no MH370 application was found. The AF447 precedent: the Australian JRCC paper to ICAO (O26, Aug 2014) records that satellite imagery gave no useful results for AF447 (civil and military images used) and a similar experience for MH370; for AF447, cloud also prevented US satellite help (S22).

**(c) Debris.** Fully covered in the register and the 17 Oct note. New this session: (i) ICAO WP30 lists the time needed for satellite-imagery analysis as a factor delaying tasking (O26, p.2); (ii) DigitalGlobe says its constellation imaged >3 million km2 per day but could not be searched without a location (S05) and that the southern Indian Ocean became a search area only days before 20 Mar (S04); (iii) a CNN report says ATSB negotiated release of the native Pléiades images from the French MoD and received them on 2 Mar 2017, whereas GA records receipt on 23 Mar 2017 (S19 vs O14; unresolved); (iv) Airbus reportedly also used TerraSAR-X and SPOT over the search area (S12, S13; grade C, no scene identified).

**(d) Prior coincidence analyses.** None found (see §1 item 4). Official reports never list which satellites were overhead, when, or with what field of regard.

Acquisition-plan logs: DLR describes an automatic mission-planning timeline built from user orders (R01) but no operator publishes acquisition plans or failed-tasking logs. Catalogue entries are therefore the only public record that an acquisition happened (inference).

## 4. What has NOT been done

1. No published orbital coincidence analysis of imaging satellites against the MH370 flight posterior (17:00-00:19 UTC) or the impact area on 8-15 Mar 2014 (provisional).
2. No agency has published imaged footprints or non-detection maps for any 2014 satellite imagery (also noted 17 Oct).
3. No public query of the SAR archives (RADARSAT-2, TerraSAR-X/TanDEM-X, COSMO-SkyMed, RISAT-1, KOMPSAT-5, HJ-1C) for 7-15 Mar 2014 over the 7th arc. The parent's archive check ({{artifact:b67917e3-fe42-4359-b830-fa16280b8630}}) found these catalogues login-gated.
4. No application of the 2024-2026 airborne-target SAR methods (A01-A03) to any 2014 scene; this needs complex (SLC/SSC/SCS) or raw data, not detected GRD/MGD products.
5. No published statement from MDA/CSA, DLR, ISRO/NRSC or KARI on MH370 tasking (S16, S17).
6. The US infrared "no explosion" statements were never extended, in any public source, to the 00:19 end of flight in the southern Indian Ocean.
7. No FOI or Senate question on AGO tasking requests, satellite footprints or Five Eyes imagery was found.

**Debris windows.** For every catalogue: footprint 70-110 E, 45-20 S; acquisition 2014-03-08T00:00Z to 2014-03-16T00:00Z (and 16-28 Mar); all modes; request SLC/SSC/SCS (complex) products, not only detected imagery. Already answered (public): Landsat 8, EO-1, ASTER (parent archive report). Login-gated, not yet queried: EODMS, EOWEB, CLEOS, Maxar Discover/Hub, Airbus OneAtlas (full archive, not Living Library), Bhoonidhi, CRESDA, KARI.

**Government holdings.** ATSB holds the four native Pléiades images and GA holds per-image metadata (O07, O14); AGO (Department of Defence) holds the AMSA imagery requests of ~15 Mar 2014 and the imagery received (O02). Routes: written request to ATSB; FOI to Defence for AGO tasking requests, satellite footprints and image lists for 7-28 Mar 2014; Senate Estimates question via a senator. Malaysia: MRSA (now MYSA) for the full 122-object list. France: DRM/CNES via ATSB for acquisition times and footprints of the 20-23 Mar radar and optical detections.
