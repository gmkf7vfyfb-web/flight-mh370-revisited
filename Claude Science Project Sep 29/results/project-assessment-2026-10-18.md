# MH370 project: an outside reading of where it stands, what it is missing, and whether the case has a solution

Claude Science, 18 October 2026. Sources read for this note: branch `claude-science-sep29` at commit 0a7e69a (17 Oct); `ARCHITECTURE.md`; the coordination inboxes through 18 Oct 04:20Z; `results/evidence-ladder.md`; `results/best-model.md`; `results/composer-views-run-j.md` (version 2, 18 Oct) and its `products.csv` on the exchange; `results/evidence-sweep-2026-10-17/evidence-register.md`; `results/kadri-haixun-conditional-2026-10-18/SYNTHESIS.md`; `results/hydroacoustics-coverage.md`; `paper/proposed-draft/README.md`; the 18 September review manuscript on Google Drive; and the public record of the Ocean Infinity 2025–26 search. Every number below is quoted from one of those files. Nothing new was computed except the table in §2, which is a re-tabulation of `products.csv`.

## 1. Where the project actually stands

The four-stage estimator described in `ARCHITECTURE.md` is built and running end to end. Core run J (24 waypoint seeds, four route families, 74 seeds in all) has been handed to end of flight, settling, drift, searched areas, Pléiades and hydroacoustics, and the composer has produced §0 views (evidence alone, core + each factor, leave-one-out, all) for six 00:19 data treatments. Agreement with Davey Fig. 10.3 is documented in two forms: the pure reproduction without the fuel model (median 38.16 S, overlap 0.717, split-half 0.934; `evidence-ladder.md`), and the fuel-plus-tempering run, which lands closest to Davey (median 37.583 S against Davey's 37.532 S, overlap 0.7825) but does not meet the convergence floor (`best-model.md`). The evidence ladder shows that the model generalises: a posterior fitted only through 00:11 predicts the held-out 00:19 arc to 17 km RMS against 6.4 km of measurement noise. That is the best single validation result in the repository and it should lead the paper's results section.

Three housekeeping facts a reviewer would trip over:

- The `ARCHITECTURE.md` table headed "Build status, honestly" still says stages 2–3 are empty frames and stage 4 does not exist. It is more than two weeks out of date and contradicts the rest of the file. Replace it or delete it.
- `paper/proposed-draft` is built on composer pass 2 on run I. Run J supersedes run I and the headline numbers moved (e.g. all-evidence median 36.07 S with Ocean Infinity). The draft needs a regeneration pass, not an edit.
- Run J's convergence is a PASS by project ruling (16 Oct). The record (`results/run-j/README.md`) gives the free-flight family a single-split value of 0.840 and an all-partition mean of 0.873 against the 8-replicate floor of 0.924, with no floor calibrated beyond 8 replicates, and says "do not label run J not converged". The paper needs a sentence that says this. Run J's recorded limitation is not the 19:41 bottleneck of the earlier evidence-ladder runs; it is the Monte-Carlo-limited shape of the PDF north of 33 S (prediction 5 failed: pooled northern lineages 120 for waypoints and 81 for free against about 450 predicted), which is what the northern supplement now running on deskstar addresses. *(Corrected 18 Oct: the earlier text quoted the 4-replicate floor and attributed the ladder runs' 19:41 bottleneck to run J.)*
- Two different family-B shares are in circulation for the same 00:19 treatment. The production two-factor table that the composer uses (`eof-run-j-oct17`) gives B 0.48 under D0' and 0.40 under D4; the joined V2 planned-descent product (`eof-run-j-joined-v2-oct17`, 24 seeds, evidence-pooled) gives 0.160 and 0.041. They are different products with different onset priors, and end of flight's own file warns that the production shares are "mostly the module prior from the 00:11 hand-off, not an A-against-B test". The paper must quote one, label it, and explain the other.
- The drift, settling and hydroacoustics hypotheses live on their own branches (`hypothesis/debris-drift` d51d80a, `hypothesis/settling` 9330d4c, `hypothesis/hydroacoustics` 77b4b49) and are not merged into `claude-science-sep29`; a reader of the working branch sees eight directories under `engine/hypotheses/` (altitude-prior, antenna-gain, arc-kernel, end-of-flight, initial-mach, pleiades, radar-fix, seabed-search) and none for drift, settling or hydroacoustics. The composer exists as `crates/compose` on the working branch, but the runner still rejects a `[[compose]]` section (`main.rs:71`); the run J views were produced by the composer's numpy scripts.

## 2. What the integrated estimate says, read plainly

From `products.csv` (run J, four-stratum mixture, ρ = 0.05, Ocean Infinity included, grade C coverage). "On searched ground" is the posterior mass inside the union of ATSB Phase 2, Bluefin-21, Ocean Infinity 2018 and Ocean Infinity 2025–26, weighted by coverage fraction. "Next OI" is the mass inside the inferred next Ocean Infinity block, reported as a map layer, not evidence.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D0 held out | core | 37.13 S | 38.41–33.87 | 162k | 0.032 | 0.771 | 0.005 |
| D0 held out | all evidence | 36.07 S | 38.54–28.47 | 437k | 0.152 | 0.239 | 0.024 |

| D3 both BFOs as recorded | core | 37.31 S | 38.03–34.74 | 57k | 0.015 | 0.947 | 0.000 |
| D3 both BFOs as recorded | all evidence | 36.64 S | 37.96–28.05 | 128k | 0.095 | 0.572 | 0.003 |
| D4 both BFOs, Holland offset | core | 37.24 S | 37.99–34.67 | 51k | 0.019 | 0.963 | 0.000 |
| D4 both BFOs, Holland offset | all evidence | 36.74 S | 37.94–26.54 | 155k | 0.127 | 0.664 | 0.005 |

Two things stand out, and neither is stated this bluntly anywhere in the repository.

**(a) Under the 00:19 BFO treatments, the integrated posterior says the wreck is most probably inside ground that has already been searched.** With both 00:19 BFOs admitted, the core puts 95–96 % of its mass on searched ground; after the search term is applied, 57–66 % remains there. The posterior is therefore saying, in effect, "the aircraft is near 37 S within the searched strip and was missed". That statement is carried entirely by two judgement parameters: the detection rating q (0.9–0.945) and ρ (0.05), which together let a fully-covered cell keep about 10–15 % of its prior weight (ρ + (1 − ρ)(1 − q) = 0.10 at q 0.945, 0.145 at q 0.9). The composer's own ρ sweep shows ρ moving the median by 0.7° and the northern mass by a factor of 1.5. Searched areas now carry 0.51 of the KL divergence between core and all evidence, which the composer flags. In other words the evidence type with the greatest influence on the answer is the one with the crudest model: a scalar q, a scalar ρ and a coverage fraction inferred at grade C.

**(b) The all-evidence region is governed by the choice of 00:19 treatment and by the Australian non-recovery term, not by the satcom.** The 90 % area runs from 51k km² (core, D4) to 593k km² (all evidence with the Australian term, D0); the median from 37.3 S to 32.2 S. The satcom has done its work; what remains is model uncertainty about the end of flight and about non-detection, and those are the things that should be on the front page of the paper.

## 3. The clues, ranked by how much they actually constrain

1. **The BFO decides the hemisphere.** Measured in this engine, not assumed: the northern-hemisphere mass goes from 22 % (BTO only) to 0.00 % when the BFO is added (`evidence-ladder.md`). Robust to everything tried.
2. **The BTO arcs fix range; fuel fixes the southern end.** Fuel and endurance alone give a disc of 47° radius; the arcs collapse it to a line thousands of kilometres long; the run J crossings of the 7th arc reach no further south than 40.6 S. The held-out 00:19 arc is predicted to 17 km. The along-arc position is where everything else is spent.
3. **The 00:19 log-on, if its BFOs are trusted, puts the aircraft in a rapid descent and therefore close to the arc.** This is the hinge of the whole case. With D3/D4, 95–96 % of the core mass lands on searched ground, which is the strip along the arc from 40 S to 25 S. The effect on the piloted-glide family is smaller than I first wrote: in the production tables B goes from 0.48 (D0') to 0.40 (D4), and end of flight warns those shares are mostly the module prior; what the 00:19 BFOs do remove is the pre-00:11 onset part of B (0.116 → 0.000 in the joined V2 product, where total B goes 0.160 → 0.041). *(Corrected 18 Oct.)* Everything downstream depends on whether the two 00:19 BFO values are treated as measurements of descent rate or as artefacts of a restarting oscillator. The project carries both (D3 against D4, C1 against C2) and that is right; the paper should say that this one choice moves more of the answer than any ocean model.
4. **The seabed searches are the strongest disconfirming evidence and the weakest-modelled.** ATSB: about 120,000 km² from 40 S to about 33 S; Ocean Infinity: about 140,000 km² from 36 S to 25 S; the 2025–26 season added about 7,571 km² and found nothing; Ocean Infinity's own statement is "it isn't where we looked". If that statement were taken at face value (ρ → 0, q → 1), the integrated posterior would be the *residual* distribution: the glide tail of family B beyond 25 NM, the strip north of 25 S, and the holidays in the sonar coverage. The project's soft values (q 0.9, ρ 0.05) stop that from happening, Phase 2's q of 0.945 comes from the ATSB's own published confidence figure (ATSB 2017 Fig. 73) and the 0.9 elsewhere is Stone et al.'s planning cap; ρ has no published value anywhere. *(Corrected 18 Oct: the earlier text said none of these values was derived from data.)*
5. **Debris drift says "north of 37 S", not where.** Drift alone has its median at 28 S and an evidence ratio against the core of only −0.5 nat; it favours the 26–33 S plateau broadly. The Western Australia non-recovery term is the strongest drift statement (−2.1 nat) and also the most model-dependent (reporting probabilities by coast tier). It is correctly carried as a labelled sensitivity.
6. **Hydroacoustics is, so far, a null.** The module contributes nothing by ruling (no detection probability). The pre-registered Kadri/Haixun conditional comes out disfavoured: timing and bearing alone give −5.6 nat under the rate background, and settling shows that Kadri's straight-line source and a Haixun beacon are not jointly compatible (no impact puts a recorder within 2 km of the Haixun contact). Royer's OHASISBIO report, as released through journalism in July 2026, records no convincing event near the arc; one of its hydrophones (NEAMS) was not recovered and its record is lost. The module's own coverage note says the absolute transmission loss and its slope are not validated, and nothing below 13 Hz is measured at H01W. The paper should state hydroacoustics as a bounded null with a value-of-information argument, which the draft already does.
7. **Pléiades is conditional and carries no Bayes factor for identity.** Correct as handled. Note that the non-imagery evidence raises the odds of H by only +0.4 nat.
8. **Everything else (WSPR, received power, radar non-detections, contrails, simulator data) is either shown to carry no information or has not been built.** The 17 Oct register ranks these and I agree with its order, with one change below.

## 4. What is missing

Not a repeat of the evidence register, which already lists the data types. These are the inferential gaps.

1. **A spatially resolved detection model for the searched areas.** This is the largest asymmetry in the project: the term with half the influence has a scalar model. What is needed per cell: sonar system and resolution (Bluefin-21, towed ProSAS/EdgeTech, Hugin AUV), terrain class (the ATSB report distinguishes terrain where detection was degraded), the ATSB's own published coverage holidays and re-survey areas, and the two 19th-century shipwrecks as positive controls (register item E1). ρ should become a field ρ(y), not a constant, and q should be derived from the positive controls and from Stone et al.'s AF447 revision of a prior search's detection probability, which is the exact precedent: AF447 was found after the detection probability of a previously searched zone was revised downward. Until this exists, the headline "on searched ground 0.57–0.66" is a statement about ρ and q, not about the ocean floor.
2. **An end-to-end calibration test.** Rule 8 requires a synthetic-recovery test per module; nothing tests the composed chain. Draw synthetic cases from the prior, push each through core → end of flight → drift and search terms at reduced scale, and check that the 50/90/95 % regions contain the truth at the stated rate. The composer thread's design (18 Oct) corrects two things in my first statement: 20–50 cases is too few (50 cannot separate 90 % from 80 % coverage; 100 is the minimum), and a same-model test checks the computation, not the model — a wrong-model stress tier is needed for the "false precision" charge the September review manuscript made against the project's own earlier work. A Journal of Navigation referee will ask for both.
3. **A dependence audit of the composed factors.** The composer multiplies likelihoods assuming conditional independence given the impact. The two ocean models are marginalised jointly, which is right, but the drift finds, the Australian non-recovery term, the 2014 surface search (when built) and the Pléiades transport all share the same GLORYS/ERA5 error realisation, and settling and the search term share the same seabed displacement. The Kadri synthesis says "adding the terms assumes they are independent (approximation)". One shared ocean-error latent, marginalised across all surface terms, or at least a sensitivity in which the ocean model is held fixed across terms, would show how much of the 437k km² is real.
4. **The arc-wide sample set.** The composer's §0 principle (each evidence type on a prior uniform along the arc) is only as good as the sample north of 33 S: effective sample 287 for all evidence, 72 for searches alone, 10 for Pléiades. The northern supplement is designed, pre-flighted and queued behind the drift chain. Nothing north of 30 S should be quoted until it lands.
5. **A decomposition of the family evidence, and a prior-volume sensitivity on it.** *(Corrected 18 Oct after Pete's objection; the earlier text wrongly implied that pilot inputs after the final major turn are not sampled, and proposed a stratum that would have forced a change, which the project's own rule forbids.)* The core does sample later pilot inputs — three manoeuvre clocks with a Jeffreys prior on τ, undirected turns, speed and altitude changes — and the run J waypoints family contains northern solutions (743 of 48,000 stored paths end north of 30 S, by a ~105° left turn at 23:11–23:51 and an increase to about 490 kt). The open question is not reach but weight attribution. The waypoints family holds ≥ 0.996 of every composed product, so the northern tail of the mixture is that family's northern tail; a family's log evidence is best fit minus an Occam penalty, and the penalty depends on prior volume (τ range, the ±180° turn prior, speed and altitude ranges, waypoint-region extent). Two cheap, non-forcing checks: (a) per family, report max ln L beside ln Z so the Occam factor is visible, and re-run the family evidence under labelled prior-volume sensitivities (τ range ×3 and ÷3, the turn-angle prior, the waypoint region extent), reporting the northern mass under each; (b) decompose ln L by epoch for the stored northern paths against the southern ones, so the paper can state which measurements penalise the north (e.g. the 00:11 BFO, the 22:41–00:11 BTO spacing) and how much of that penalty rests on the BFO error model.
6. **The 2014 aerial surface search as non-detection evidence (register A1, with E3 as its prerequisite).** CSIRO's strongest argument against latitudes north of about 33 S is this term, and it is also the one that would most directly test the drift module's northern plateau. It was deferred on a licence question about the search polygons; Pete ruled on 18 Oct that the project has rights to the 2014 search polygons (recorded in the searched-areas work order), so the blocker is gone and the term needs a thread.
7. **A decision product for the 2026–27 season.** Malaysia has extended Ocean Infinity's contract to 30 June 2027 with the remaining ~7,400 km² of the priority area; work resumes about November. The project already computes Davey eq. 11.2 (blocks ranked by probability of success at a planning detection probability). Under the current posterior the planned next block holds 0.5–5.5 % of the mass depending on treatment. A ranked block list with its sensitivity to ρ and to the 00:19 treatment is the single most useful thing this project can publish before November, and it is a figure the paper can carry.
8. **Reporting hygiene for the paper.** The results are presented per 00:19 treatment and per family, which is right. What is still missing is one table that says, for each treatment, what fraction of the posterior is (i) on searched ground, (ii) in the glide tail beyond 25 NM, (iii) north of 25 S, because those three cells are the three physically distinct explanations for twelve years of non-detection and a reader wants to see them side by side.

## 5. Is there a solution?

In the sense of a bounded region, yes, and the project has it: under every treatment the 90 % region lies on the 7th arc between about 39 S and 25–28 S, within a few tens of nautical miles of the arc, with 437k km² at 90 % when the 00:19 data are held out and 128–155k km² when both 00:19 BFOs are used. North of the equator is excluded to the precision of 56 million particles; south of 40 S is excluded by fuel.

In the sense of a searchable area for one season (roughly 10,000–20,000 km²), no, and more particles will not change that. The satcom evidence has been exhausted: its peak near 37 S has been searched to ±25 NM and the remaining mass along the arc is shaped by nuisance parameters, not by measurements. The posterior is now a statement about three things the project has not yet measured: how good the searches really were (ρ, q, per cell), whether the 00:19 BFOs are descent-rate measurements, and whether the ocean-model errors shared across the drift terms are large. Each of those is a tractable piece of work and none of them is a new flight-dynamics run.

The honest shape of the answer, under the current model, is a three-way split that should be stated as such: (i) the wreck is inside searched ground near 36–38 S and was missed (the posterior's modal explanation under D3/D4, and entirely ρ- and q-dependent); (ii) the wreck is in the piloted-glide tail beyond the searched strip at 34–39 S (not yet tabulated: the B family holds 0.40–0.48 of the production mixture, but most B impacts lie inside the searched strip, and the fraction beyond it is exactly the number the three-way split table must supply); (iii) the wreck is on the unsearched arc north of about 25–28 S (10–15 % of the all-evidence mass, rising to half with the Australian term). The data in hand cannot choose among them. The work in §4, items 1, 2, 3 and 6, is what would.

## 6. Three things I would do first

1. Build the spatial detection model for searched areas (q and ρ per cell from the ATSB coverage record and the shipwreck positive controls) and re-run the composer views. This moves the answer more than anything else in the queue.
2. Run the end-to-end calibration test at smoke scale. It is cheap, it is what the paper needs most, and it will find errors nothing else will.
3. Publish the ranked-block decision table for the 2026–27 season, with its sensitivity to ρ and the 00:19 treatment, in time for the November resumption.
