# 2014 aerial and surface search for MH370: source ledger for the searched-areas module

Compiled 17 Oct 2026; every access date is 17 Oct 2026 unless stated. Scope: the 2014 aerial and surface search and other non-seabed searches, with a focus on the southern Indian Ocean.

**Grades.** A = peer-reviewed or official primary source, read with page or section locators. B = official, cited but not read in full. C = online or community analysis, news, or inferred geometry.

**How the documents were obtained.**
- www.atsb.gov.au accepted connections from this sandbox but never answered an HTTP request. The JACC domain no longer exists (proxy 502).
- Official PDFs were therefore read from one of three places:
  1. copies already on Pete's Google Drive;
  2. Internet Archive captures of the original official URLs (capture date given);
  3. the live site, where it answered.
- AMSA's 2014 search-chart download links now return "access denied" from AMSA itself.
- The 41 MB ATSB *Operational Search* PDF exceeds the Drive connector's 10 MB limit. It was read as the Drive text extraction, which keeps the printed page footers (printed page numbers are quoted below). The daily table columns are collapsed in that extraction (see 4.5).

- **Access.** Original URL https://www.atsb.gov.au/media/5773565/operational-search-for-mh370_final_3oct2017.pdf. Read from Pete's Drive copy (41.7 MB) via text extraction. Grade A.
- **Early searches (printed pp.15–19).** All three areas are shown only as annotated Google Earth images (Figs 6, 7 and 9).

- **AMSA-led search (pp.19–33).**
  - A drift working group was formed (AMSA, CSIRO, APASA, USCG, BoM, GEMS); drift was checked with SLDMBs (pp.19–20).
  - Daily tables: Table 10 (18–19 Mar, p.23); Table 12 (20–27 Mar, p.24); Table 14 (28 Mar–3 Apr, p.28); Table 16 (3–7 Apr, p.31); Table 17 (8–17 Apr, p.32); Table 18 (18–28 Apr, p.33).
  - Table columns: date, drifted splash-point area, search area (km²), track spacing (NM), aircraft, vessels, [sonobuoy drop area], SLDMB drops, sightings.
  - Area A, 28 Mar: more than 50 objects sighted; none associated with MH370 (p.27).
  - The search ran 18 Mar–28 Apr (42 days), with 21 aircraft and 19 vessels from 8 nations (p.33). Over thirty items were recovered by ships, all considered unlikely to be from MH370 (p.33).
  - Daily coverage up to 31 Mar is referred to the AMSA website (p.33). That page is now a text timeline only.
- **"Surface search coverage and confidence" (pp.34–35).**
  - AMSA analysed daily coverage confidence from coverage reports and visibility. Confidence was low on some days because of weather, or because the area was large for the assets available.
  - Fig. 19 shows cumulative coverage. CSIRO re-assessed effectiveness for impact sites from 21°S to 41°S and for a range of leeways.
  - The first days were "fairly ineffective". The 28 Mar move north gave a high probability of detecting debris for impacts between about 26°S and 32.5°S. The report therefore judges a seafloor search north of about 32.5°S unlikely to succeed (p.34).
  - Fig. 20 (p.35) is the CSIRO probability-of-detection map.
- **Satellite imagery (pp.116–119).**
  - Imagery was requested around 15 Mar from France, Italy, Germany, Thailand, China and the US.
  - Possible debris at 43–45°S, 90–97°E was searched by aircraft on 20–27 Mar without result.
  - Possible debris at 34–35.5°S, 90–92°E (20–23 Mar, radar and optical) was "never searched by air"; only transiting merchant vessels were asked to look out (p.117).
  - The 23 Mar Pleiades images were taken in an area not searched by aircraft (p.118). Geoscience Australia found 70 objects in them, 12 probably man-made (p.119).
- **Other points.** p.121: in choppy seas with breaking waves, floating debris is harder to detect. p.113 restates the CSIRO conclusion that debris from north of 32°S would probably have been detected.
- **Not provided:** daily POD values, sweep widths or polygon coordinates.

### A2. Griffin DA, Oke PR, Jones EM (2016). *The search for MH370 and ocean surface drift.* CSIRO Oceans and Atmosphere, report EP167888, 8 Dec 2016 (released 20 Dec 2016). doi:10.4225/08/5892224dec08c.
- **Access.** http://www.atsb.gov.au/media/5772119/mh370_ocean_driftv29.pdf. Read from Pete's Drive copy. Grade A.
- **Executive summary (p.iv).** Debris from north of 32°S "would probably have been detected by the surface search". Integer latitudes carry an implied uncertainty of ±0.5°.
- **Section 4, "The March–April 2014 sea-surface search" (p.23).**
  - AMSA coordinators made daily POD estimates from weather, number of assets, search-area size and other factors.
  - These estimates assume a single target, so the authors call them "conservative".
  - Result: the search "strongly contra-indicates regions north of 32°S" and mildly contra-indicates 32–34°S. 34–38°S is where debris was highly likely to have escaped detection.
- **Fig. 4.1 (p.24).** Daily polygons (left) and the cumulative POD map (right). Items start within 45 NM of the 7th arc with 1.2% windage, in the BRAN2015 model. A flaperon case is in the online appendix.
- **Conclusion (p.25).** The surface search "argues quite strongly" that the site was not between 32°S and 25°S.

### A5. Griffin DA, Oke PR (2017c). *The search for MH370 and ocean surface drift – Part IV.* CSIRO EP177204, 3 Oct 2017 (the citation page says August 2017).
- **Access.** https://www.atsb.gov.au/sites/default/files/media/5773567/mh370_ocean_driftiv_oct2017.pdf. Read from Pete's Drive copy. Grade A.
- **This is the source of the statement in Pete's screenshot.** Fig. 1.1 caption (p.2): the cumulative POD "shows that the aerial search effectively ruled out crash sites north of about 33°S but ineffectively searched latitudes south of 34°S". The high-windage result is "very similar".
- **Method (pp.1–2).**
  1. Each daily search box had one POD, applied equally to every item inside it, whatever the item's position relative to the flown tracks.
  2. That POD was low (10% or 20%) on days of very poor weather or track spacing over 20 NM, and high (often 100%) on other days.
  3. Each drifting parcel's cumulative POD is 1 − ∏(1 − POD_day); for example, two boxes at 50% give 75%.
  4. The map plots the spatially binned arithmetic mean over adjacent parcels.
  5. The authors note these values would over-estimate detection of single items; they are meant to represent detection of a debris field.
- **Section 2 (p.3): the 30°S example.**
  - The debris field for a 30°S impact was targeted from 28 Mar to 1 Apr by 8–11 aircraft at 12–16 NM track spacing; several carried marine surveillance radar. Daily areas were 164,087–298,636 km².
  - Both ocean models (BRAN2015 and BRAN2016) show the field overflown several times, but they disagree on which tracks came closest. The authors conclude single-item POD "cannot reliably be estimated".
  - Their alternative argument: expected detections ≈ N·P·W/S per day, where W = 2R. With N = 200, P = 0.5 and W/S = 0.1 this gives 10 per day, or 50 over 5 days. Halving N and W gives 12.
- **Conclusion (p.6).** There was a "fairly high chance" of detection, at least for the Broken Ridge section searched 28 Mar–1 Apr. The authors recommend three further steps:
  1. field measurement of radar detection ranges for real debris (e.g. the flaperon);
  2. repeating the analysis for other locations and other drift models;
  3. high-resolution dispersion modelling.
  No evidence was found that any of these was done.
- **Data.** Four kmz files of modelled items for an impact at 30.5°S, 97.86°E (BRAN2015/2016 × 1.2%/3% windage), downloaded.

### A10. Australia (2014). *MH370 Search and Rescue Response – JRCC Australia.* ICAO APANPIRG ATM/SG/2-WP30, Hong Kong, 4–8 Aug 2014.
- **Access.** Pete's Drive copy. Grade A.
- **Drift planning (para 2.18, p.5).** AMSA's drift tool is Net Water Movement (NWM), validated against another model and against SLDMBs; 33 SLDMBs were deployed. A drift planning working group (para 2.19) and a Search Strategy Working Group (para 2.20) supported it.
- **Search totals (para 2.21, p.6).** **345 sorties, 3,177 flight hours, a cumulative 4.7 million km² and 28 aircraft.** No debris was identified (para 2.22).
- **Detection problems (para 2.2, pp.2–3).** Marine pollution confused the identification of debris (2.2(m)). The manufacturer advised that composite components were the parts most likely to float (2.2(o)).
- **Para 2.32 (p.7).** The 60,000 km² priority area was surface-searched on days 21–26.
- **Attachment 1 (pp.9–11): comparison with AF447.** The initial area was 693,170 km² and the cumulative area "almost 4.7 million" km². 33 datum buoys were used, against 9 for AF447. AF447 debris was first found on day 5, about 70 km from the last known position.

### A17. Stone LD, Keller C, Kratzke TL, Strumpfer J (2011). *Search Analysis for the Location of the AF447 Underwater Wreckage.* Metron report to the BEA, 20 Jan 2011.
- **Access.** Pete's Drive copy. Grade A.
- **This is the precedent for treating an aerial and surface search as negative information (section 4.2, pp.12–17).**
  - The 1–6 June 2009 French and Brazilian sorties were reconstructed in SAROPS from altitude, speed, aircraft type, visibility and sea state.
  - The detection target was a **4-person life raft, used as a surrogate for large debris such as the galley**.
  - Particles were drifted, and each particle's weight was multiplied by (1 − POD) for every sortie that passed over it.
  - High-altitude radar patrols (R-99, E-3F) were not credited. Some Brazilian sorties were left out for lack of detail.
- **pp.17–18.** The resulting surface-search posterior was given **only 30% weight**, mixed with 70% prior, because it depends on low-confidence estimates of the currents.

### A18. Ivić S et al. (2020). Search strategy in a complex and dynamic environment: the MH370 case. *Scientific Reports* 10:19640, doi:10.1038/s41598-020-76274-0.
- **Access.** Fetched by DOI (open access). Grade A.
- **p.1.** The surface search covered more than 4.6 million km² (citing ATSB).
- **pp.6–7: a replication of the actual search.**
  - Inputs: the JACC areas searched in a lawnmower pattern, by aircraft only, at 380 km/h for 3 hours a day.
  - Detection: a target within 1.5 km is detected with P = 1 − exp(−t / 2 s).
  - Result: **33.7% of targets detected for drifted Area A**, against 43.8% for a lawnmower search of the model-drifted area.
  - Ships were excluded. Results are averages over 100 realisations of 1,000 targets.

## 4. Findings for the likelihood

**4.1 The screenshot claim, verified.**
- The wording "the aerial search effectively ruled out crash sites north of about 33°S but ineffectively searched latitudes south of 34°S" is the caption of **Part IV Fig. 1.1 (p.2)**.
- The same caption, without "about", appears in Part III Fig. 1.2 (p.2). The equivalent finding for 3% windage is in Part II Fig. 4.2 (p.16).
- The stated boundary moves between documents:

  | Source | Stated boundary |
  |---|---|
  | Part I (pp.iv, 23, 25) and First Principles Review (p.18) | North of 32°S; "not between 32°S and 25°S" |
  | Part II (p.15) | Very likely seen for 27–32°S |
  | ATSB 2017 (p.34) | About 26–32.5°S |
  | CSIRO figure captions | North of (about) 33°S |

- **No text says "almost certainly".** The strongest wording is "effectively ruled out". Part IV p.3 calls the probability of detecting at least one item "very high, for this example location".

**4.2 Method behind the claim.**
1. AMSA's daily POD, estimated for a single target, was applied equally to every item inside that day's box: 0.1–0.2 on very poor days or where track spacing exceeded 20 NM, and often 1.0 otherwise.
2. The cumulative POD is 1 − ∏(1 − POD_day).
3. Parcels start within 45 NM of the arc and drift in BRAN2015 with 1.2% or 3% windage.
4. Results are binned on 0.5° and averaged over adjacent parcels.

**The daily POD values are not published.** The days set to 1.0 drive the conclusion. Part IV supports them with an expected-count argument (N·P·W/S) for a single example site at 30.5°S.

**4.4 Coverage, platforms and sweep width.**
- **Track spacing by period:**
  - 18–20 Mar: 40 NM, far south.
  - 21–27 Mar: 2–5 NM, in small boxes at 43–46°S.
  - 28 Mar–5 Apr: 12–16 NM, about 28–33°S; these are the decisive days.
  - 3–7 Apr: 9–20 NM.
  - 8–28 Apr: 3–7 NM.
- **Single-item coverage.** With the IAMSAR example sweep width W = 2.3 NM, coverage at 12–16 NM spacing is C ≈ 0.14–0.19 per day. Part IV used W/S = 0.1. Under the random-search law, single-item POD is about 1 − e^(−C) ≈ 0.10–0.17 per day.
- **Field-level detection depends on N_eff,** the effective number of independent detectable items. With p = 0.1 per day for 5 days:
  - N_eff = 10 gives P(all missed) ≈ 0.005;
  - N_eff = 2 gives P(all missed) ≈ 0.35.
- The Ivić et al. replication detects 34% of individual targets in Area A.
- The military aircraft carried surface-search radar, but **no radar sweep width for small debris has been published**; CSIRO itself recommended measuring one.

**4.5 Conflicts between sources.**
- **Total area.** More than 4.5 million km² (PM); 4.7 million (ICAO); 4.6 million (Ivić et al., citing ATSB); 4,274,982 km² as the sum of the ATSB daily tables (computed here); "several million" (Malaysian SIR).
  - Note that ATSB Table 7 gives 4,560,000 km² for the separate Andaman/Malacca search.
- **Flights and assets.** 334 flights and 29 aircraft (PM); 345 sorties and 28 aircraft (ICAO); 21 aircraft and 19 vessels (ATSB p.33).
- **Daily areas.**
  - 18 Mar: 65,938 km² (ATSB) against 76,000 (AMSA).
  - 19 Mar: 320,855 against 305,000 / 304,000.
  - 28 Mar: 298,636 against 256,000 searched (AMSA Update 25) and 319,000 planned.
  - In April, ATSB and the morning JACC figure agree to within 0.1% on 9, 10, 12, 14, 21 and 25 Apr. ATSB is lower on days of bad weather. This suggests ATSB records the area achieved (an inference).
- **ATSB table columns.** Rows with fewer than four trailing numbers are flagged `column_assignment_ambiguous=y`. Area and track spacing are reliable; the asset, SLDMB and sighting counts on flagged rows need checking against the PDF.
- **Number of floating items.** CSIRO assumes N ~ 100–200 detectable items. The PM argued that by day 52 most material would have sunk; this matters for the late-April days.

**4.6 Coverage holes that matter for a latitude likelihood.**
- 34–35.5°S, 90–92°E, where French satellites saw possible debris on 20–23 Mar, was **never searched by air** (ATSB p.117).
- The Pleiades area NW of the arc was not searched by aircraft (ATSB p.118).
- The CSIRO maps show "no search effort" along the arc from about 34°S to 37°S.
- The 18–20 Mar searches, at 40 NM spacing, were "fairly ineffective" (ATSB p.34).

**4.7 Precedent and suggested treatment.**
- Metron (AF447) reconstructed the sorties in SAROPS against a 4-person life raft surrogate, then gave the resulting surface-search posterior only 30% weight.
- A first latitude-only proxy for the module: L(lat) = ρ + (1 − ρ)(1 − POD_cum(lat)), with POD_cum taken from the digitised CSV.
  - ρ should carry the doubt about drift, about N_eff, and about the unpublished daily POD values.
  - Metron's 30% weight corresponds to ρ ≈ 0.7.

## Addendum (17 Oct 2026, lead session): official daily search-area polygons DO exist

After this ledger was written, the Geoscience Australia ArcGIS feature service
`Search_And_Rescue_Search_Area2014` (https://services1.arcgis.com/wfNKYeHsOyaFyPw3/arcgis/rest/services/Search_And_Rescue_Search_Area2014/FeatureServer/0)
was queried. It holds **387 polygons named by incident day, D11 (18 Mar 2014) to D52 (28 Apr 2014)**; day n = 7 Mar + n.
D11 = 75,962 km² against AMSA's day-1 chart 76,000; D12 = 306,521 km² against the chart's 304,000. D14, D18 (25 Mar weather
stand-down) and D51 are absent. Sum over days 4,025,819 km²; **union 1,726,495 km²**. Union per degree of latitude is lowest at
35–37°S (10,156 and 12,494 km²), matching CSIRO's "no search effort" band. The polygons are search boxes, not flown tracks;
they carry no POD. Grade A for geometry (official, GA/ATSB service), with the same service exposing `WideSearchArea`
(the 26 Jun 2014 ±100 NM wide area to 39°S). Files: ga_search_and_rescue_search_area_2014.geojson,
surface_search_daily_polygons_summary.csv, surface_search_union_by_latitude.csv.
The statements above that no polygon coordinates were published are superseded by this addendum.
