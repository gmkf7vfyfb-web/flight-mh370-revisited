# Seabed surveys and searches: source ledger

Scope: seabed mapping and seabed searches for MH370 (2014 to October 2026), and the detection performance of sonar against aircraft wreckage. Prepared 17 Oct 2026. Every source was accessed on 17 Oct 2026 unless stated otherwise.

Grades: **A** = official or peer-reviewed primary source, read here, with page or section locators. **B** = official, cited but not read in full here. **C** = online or community analysis, used as argument or as inferred geometry, and labelled as such.

Page locators for S01 are printed page numbers (the "› N ‹" footer). PDF page = printed page + 9 for the main body.

## S01. ATSB 2017, *The Operational Search for MH370* (A)

**Citation.** Australian Transport Safety Bureau (2017). *The Operational Search for MH370*. ATSB Transport Safety Report, External Aviation Investigation AE-2014-054, final, 3 October 2017. 440 pp.
**Copy read.** Pete's Google Drive, `operational-search-for-mh370_final_3oct2017.pdf` (fileId 1zwSCCwXcsryJdi4kHb4WE13_9-WIb4J4, 41,685,870 bytes, sha256 bb6ccea2…bcc73). Text was read through the Drive text extraction for printed pp. 1–160. Figure 73 was read from a render of PDF page 105, made from the same file (hash recorded in S27).
**Official URL.** https://www.atsb.gov.au/investigations/ae-2014-054. The ATSB site refuses automated clients (HTTP/2 reset, retested today), so it was not opened directly.
**Grade.** A for the main body. The appendices (Fugro and Phoenix contractor reports, Appendices D and E) were **not** read, because the text extraction stops after the vessel pages.

What it provides:
- **Acoustic (ULB) search, 2 to 17 April 2014 (pp. 36–41).**
  - ULBs: Dukane DK100, 37.5 kHz, 10 ms pulse every second, at least 30 days (40 days predicted). They can be detected at 2,000–3,000 m (p. 37).
  - Ocean Shield ran US Navy TPLs 03S and 01D from 4 April. There were five detections, mostly near 33 kHz and 42 kHz. These were later attributed to a fault in the TPL self-test cable (pp. 39–41). TPL coverage polygons appear only in Figure 23, which is an image.
  - Haixun 01 used a hand-held hydrophone, with detections on 4–5 April at 25.975°S 101.461°E. Its practical range was about 2,000 m (pp. 38–39).
  - HMS Echo had a hull-mounted system. Its detection was discounted (p. 38).
  - The P-3C sonobuoys could detect a ULB at up to 4,000 m and cover about 3,000 km² per sortie. They were dropped 6–17 April with no detections (p. 41).
- **Bluefin-21 / Artemis AUV, Ocean Shield (pp. 41–42).**
  - 30 missions from 14 April 2014 at 3,800 to about 5,000 m depth.
  - 120 kHz SSS at 400 m range scale and 45 m altitude.
  - Tasking: a 10 km radius around TPL detection 2 and a 3 km radius around detections 1, 3 and 4.
  - Completed 28 May 2014: 860 km², no debris.
  - No detection probability is stated.
- **Tender and sensor specification (pp. 53–59).**
  - Required detection capability: a 2 m³ object (the size of a B777 engine core).
  - AF447 debris field: about 600 m × 200 m at 3,980 m depth (p. 53).
  - Deep tow: Edgetech, 75 kHz SSS, 2.5–3 kn. Validated swath 2,000 m; data collected over 2,200 m, but the outer 100 m on each side was not counted. Line spacing 1,700 m, with up to 300 m overlap. Coverage above 200 km² per operational day (pp. 58–59).
  - AUV: same SSS, up to 3.6 kn, validated to a 2,000 m swath.
  - ProSAS: 60 kHz SAS, 10 cm resolution over the swath, 2,000 m swath at about 1.8 kn (p. 59).
  - All vehicles had MBES for the nadir, plus INS, DVL and USBL. Practical USBL accuracy is about 50 m at 8–9 km range (p. 56).
  - Downtime was 5.6% and weather standby 9.9% (p. 60).
  - Test range: 650 m depth, five steel targets from 1.3 m to 2 m in size. Reliable detection out to 1,000 m range; intermittent at 1,000–1,100 m (pp. 60–61).
- **Bathymetry (Phase 1) (pp. 44–47, 63–65).**
  - Fugro Equator, Fugro Supporter and Zhu Kezhen.
  - 278,650 km² of high-resolution MBES in the search area, and 710,000 km² including transits.
  - Mainly May–December 2014, with supplementary data to February 2017.
  - Resolution printed as "40 m2 per pixel", against about 5 km² per pixel for the earlier satellite-derived bathymetry (p. 46).
  - Area by period: Table 19 (14 Jun–26 Aug 2014), about 38,000 km² at 27.4–32.1°S. Table 21 (26 Aug–26 Sep 2014), about 14,000 km² at 32.8–35°S. Table 22, about 46,000 km² at 32.8–38.9°S.
  - GA backscatter analysis: 53 navigation features, 42 response-uncertainty areas and 608 potential targets (Table 20, p. 52).
- **Underwater search chronology (pp. 62–77).**
  - Dates: 6 Oct 2014 to 17 Jan 2017.
  - Vessels and periods:
    - GO Phoenix, ProSAS: 6 Oct 2014 to 19 Jun 2015, Search Area 1 (about 14,000 km², 32.8–35°S).
    - Fugro Discovery, deep tow: from 22 Oct 2014.
    - Fugro Equator, deep tow: from 15 Jan 2015. It used the AUV from Dec 2016 to 17 Jan 2017.
    - Fugro Supporter, Hugin AUV: 29 Jan to 11 May 2015.
    - Havila Harmony, Hugin AUV: from 5 Dec 2015.
    - Dong Hai Jiu 101, ProSAS: from Mar 2016. It carried the Remora III ROV from Oct 2016.
  - Cumulative area: about 38,000 km² (16 Apr 2015, with about 5% gaps), about 56,750 (Table 23), about 75,850 (Table 24; about 3.8% gaps at 30 Nov 2015), 113,400 (22 Jul 2016), about 115,640 (Table 25), and about 120,000 (Table 26).
  - Width across the 7th arc: −20/+30 NM, then −40/+40 NM (Table 25), and finally ±25 NM (Table 26).
- **Data quality (pp. 82–90).**
  - Debris-field size study: at least 100 m × 100 m, and very likely larger than 200 m × 200 m (p. 83).
  - Deep tow single-pass coverage was about 98%. The remaining 2% were holidays of four types: terrain avoidance (altitude above 300 m), shadow, off-track and equipment failure (pp. 86–87).
  - LPD areas were about 2% of the deep-tow area. They were rated at 50–90% likelihood that an analyst would detect the debris field, and about 48% of them were searched at least twice (pp. 87–88).
  - SAS reduced the LPD area (p. 87).
  - A 200 m × 200 m low-lying field was shown to be detectable in the SSS nadir at towfish altitudes up to 200 m (p. 89).
  - Contacts: 618 at level 3, 41 at level 2 and 2 at level 1. 82 contacts were investigated and eliminated, there were 39 ROV investigation areas, and 4 shipwrecks were found (pp. 90–93).
- **Confidence in the search results and Figure 73 (pp. 95–97).**
  - Detection-confidence ratings: gaps of all types 0%; LPD 50–90%, nominal 70%; all other area >95%.
  - Four areas:
    - Red (Indicative Search Area): 27.5 NM NW and 25 NM SE.
    - Orange (north of the ISA): 23 NM NW and 17 NM SE.
    - Yellow (furthest north): 10 NM NW and 13 NM SE.
    - Purple (outer band): 27.5–36 NM NW and 25–41 NM SE.
  - **Figure 73 (printed p. 96, PDF p. 105), "Coverage statistics and associated confidence of detection, using 100 m x 100 m data gap metric", read from the image:**
    - Red: 98.7% high, 1.1% lower, 0.2% gaps.
    - Orange: 91.5 / 7.8 / 0.7.
    - Yellow: 97.8 / 1.1 / 1.1.
    - Purple: 97.9 / 1.5 / 0.6.
    - **Combined: 97.4 / 2.1 / 0.5.**
  - Filtering out gaps and LPDs smaller than 200 m × 200 m changes coverage by only +0.1% (gaps) and +0.2% (LPD). The ATSB kept the 100 m metric (pp. 96–97).
- **Executive summary.** "In excess of 120,000 square kilometres" searched, and 710,000 km² mapped. An area of less than 25,000 km² was identified from the First Principles Review together with the Pleiades re-analysis.
- **Limitations.** Areas by vessel and by Figure 73 sub-area are not given in the text. AUV statistics (Figure 65) and TPL coverage (Figure 23) appear only as images and were not read.

# Findings

## 1. Numbers for the likelihood

**ATSB Phase 2 detection probability.**
- The value q = 0.945 is now traced. Figure 73 (S01 printed p. 96) gives area fractions of high (>95%), lower (about 70%) and gap (0%) coverage:

  | Area | High % | Lower % | Gap % | q (0.95/0.70/0) | q, low LPD (0.95/0.50) | q, high (1.0/0.90) | q given gaps already removed |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Red (ISA) | 98.7 | 1.1 | 0.2 | **0.94535** | 0.94315 | 0.9969 | 0.94724 |
  | Orange | 91.5 | 7.8 | 0.7 | 0.92385 | 0.90825 | 0.9852 | 0.93036 |
  | Yellow | 97.8 | 1.1 | 1.1 | 0.93680 | 0.93460 | 0.9879 | 0.94722 |
  | Purple | 97.9 | 1.5 | 0.6 | 0.94055 | 0.93755 | 0.9925 | 0.94623 |
  | Combined | 97.4 | 2.1 | 0.5 | **0.94000** | 0.93580 | 0.9929 | 0.94472 |

- Two readings give q ≈ 0.945:
  - the red (Indicative Search Area) value, 0.94535;
  - the combined value with gaps excluded from the footprint, 0.940/0.995 = 0.9447.
- The combined area-average value is 0.940.
- Because ">95%" is a lower bound, the plausible range for the combined area is about 0.936–0.993. Orange (north of the ISA, about 35–36°S on the figure) is noticeably weaker, at 0.924.
- Use 0.940 on a footprint that includes the gaps, or 0.945 on a footprint with the gaps cut out — never both.
- The values are area averages. The ATSB published no map of gap or LPD positions; GA's 2022 review shows that such polygons exist inside ATSB/GA.

- The only geometry is the CAPTION outline (148,903 km²), so the mean coverage inside the outline is about 0.75 if all the search lay inside it.

## 2. Conflicts between sources

1. **Phase 2 area: 120,000 km² against 104,104 km² against 121,485 km².** The ATSB says "in excess of 120,000" and "≈120,000". The GA deep-tow valid footprint is 104,104 km². The CAPTION outline is 121,485 km². GO Phoenix SAS and AUV infill make up the difference. The GA WFS records for SAS are not footprints.
2. **Bluefin-21: 860 km² (ATSB) against 769.3 km² (GA polygons).**
3. **OI 2018: 112,000 km² (OI, searched), against 120,000 km² (OI, data donated), against 148,903 km² (CAPTION outline), against 140,000 km² (OI 2026, mapped 2018–26).** Only 112,000 km² is a search statement.
4. **First Principles Review.** It reports about 97% coverage with 3% gaps, while ATSB 2017 Figure 73 reports 97.4/2.1/0.5. The two use different stages and metrics; the FPR denominator is not visible in what was read.
5. **Overall Phase 2 detection probability.** The FPR and ATSB p. 80 say ">95%" overall, while the Figure 73 nominal arithmetic gives 0.940. ">95%" is the rating for the high-confidence class, not an area average.
6. **OI 2024–25 proposal.** The proposal was 33–36°S, but much of the March 2025 work lay at 35.2–36.5°S. That is wider and further south-west than proposed (S22), and partly outside band A (Blelly-Marchand).
7. **Families' progress reports.** 7,236.40 km² by 15 Jan plus the March 2025 work gives a total near 7,571 km². The ministry's figure is the one to use.
