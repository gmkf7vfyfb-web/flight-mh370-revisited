Figure X. Spectrogram (upper panel) and 8-60 Hz power in 1 s steps (lower panel) of a 5 min composite in which six sounds are added to real ambient noise recorded by a seabed acoustic logger in the Perth Canyon, Western Australia (3 March 2014, 13:05 UTC; 250 samples per second; recorder high-pass filter at 8 Hz). From left: synthetic Antarctic blue whale and pygmy blue whale calls built from published call parameters; the recorded ocean-borne sound (T-phase) of an earthquake (Mw 6.0, Mauritius-Reunion region, 19 July 2017, recorded at Cape Leeuwin 5,087 km from the epicentre), reduced in level; synthetic iceberg harmonic tremor; and two synthetic aircraft impact signals from the source model, a steep dive and a ditching-like impact, each propagated over 1,841 km. Signal-to-noise ratio is the peak 30 s signal energy over the median 30 s noise energy in the 8-40 Hz band: whale calls -0.4 dB and -1.7 dB, earthquake +10 dB, iceberg tremor +6 dB, each impact 0 dB. Spectrogram levels are in decibels over the median noise level at each frequency (2.05 s segments). In the lower panel the grey line is the noise alone. At equal signal-to-noise ratio the two impact signals are nearly indistinguishable, because the corner frequencies of both source spectra lie far below the 8 Hz filter. The faint short tonal sounds near 66 Hz and 23 Hz are part of the recorded noise.
