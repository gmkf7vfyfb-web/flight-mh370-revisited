+# Satellite sightings of possible floating objects, southern Indian Ocean, March 2014 — evidence review (Q1–Q5)
+
+Prepared 17 Oct 2026 for the MH370 Bayesian re-analysis (Journal of Navigation paper). Official sources were read first, then peer-reviewed papers, then independent analysts (marked **[independent]**). Press reports appear only where no primary document was found (marked **[press]**). Values were copied from the documents named. Anything not found is marked **NOT FOUND**.
+
+## Scope limits and deviations (read first)
+
+- **Historical TLEs were not obtained.** Space-Track (`gp_history`) needs an account login, no credential is configured, and the sandbox did not reach www.space-track.org. CelesTrak returns only current (2026) elements. Overpass times in Q2 are therefore **nominal sun-synchronous estimates** from published node local times. They are not SGP4 propagations of 2014 elements. They are checked against the two acquisition times that were published, and they agree (§Q2.3).
+- **Commercial catalogues were not queried.** Maxar Discover, Airbus OneAtlas, CRESDA and GISTDA all need registration. Their contents (catalogue IDs, footprints) are **NOT FOUND / not tested**.
+- **THEOS coordinates are provisional.** They were read from graticule labels on 494×700-pixel archived thumbnails. Two independent distance checks support them (§Q1 notes).
+- The ATSB *Operational Search* report (41 MB) was read as extracted text from the project Google Drive copy. Page numbers are given as PDF page / printed page.
+- The Malaysian SIR 2018 main report and the *Factual Information* (2015) were searched in full text. Neither discusses the 2014 satellite imagery of floating objects. The SIR appendices folder was **not searched**.
+- The Metabunk, Weather Graphics and 370location pages were read directly or from Internet Archive captures. PPRuNe was **not read**, because the forum thread was not retrieved.

+## Q1. Published positions, sizes, counts, times, sensors
+
+### Q1 short answer
+Five of the six sighting sets have published positions. Two of them have a published acquisition time to the second or minute:
+- the 16 March DigitalGlobe image, **04:16 UTC**, printed on the Australian Defence image sheets;
+- the 24 March THEOS image, **03:07:56.153985 UTC**, printed on the GISTDA image sheets.
+
+**Gaofen-1 (18 Mar):** one position and size, from China's SASTIND as reported. The time is reported only as "around noon" or "early", so the exact time is NOT FOUND.
+
+**Airbus 23 Mar (the 122 objects):** this is **not** the same scene set as the four French Pléiades images near 35° S that Geoscience Australia analysed. Malaysia's MRSA published 11 object positions with sizes near **44.68° S, 90.53° E**, in the same southern area as the 16–18 March sightings.
+
+**Japan (26 Mar):** about 10 objects within a 10 km radius of 44°17′ S 90°56′ E, imaged between 00:00 and 06:00 UTC by an "information-gathering satellite". Japan did not release the images or name the satellite.
+
+All of these positions are 2,500–2,770 km south-west of Perth, except the four GA Pléiades scenes (34.4–35.4° S).

+## Q2. Can pass times and footprints be found or computed?
+
+### Q2 short answer
+**Partly.** Two acquisition times are published to the minute or better (DigitalGlobe 04:16 UTC; THEOS 03:07:56 UTC). No image footprint polygon has been published by any agency. Only the GA Pléiades scene corners can be estimated (scene size and spacing [S8 p.8]; CSIRO and ATSB figures).
+
+Historical TLEs for March 2014 exist on Space-Track (`gp_history` class, free account) for every unclassified platform listed below. The Japanese IGS spacecraft are the exception: CelesTrak's catalogue lists IGS 5A, 6A and 7A with no orbit parameters. Space-Track could not be reached or logged into from here, so the SGP4 step was not run.
+
+A nominal sun-synchronous model reproduces both published times to within the off-nadir pointing implied by each image:
+- For THEOS, the model puts the nadir track 339 km east of the target. The published 27.0° incidence angle implies a 367 km ground offset.
+- For DigitalGlobe, the model puts the nadir track 458 km west of the target. This is consistent with an off-nadir collection.
+
+So the method works. Exact footprints need either the catalogue metadata (Maxar, Airbus, GISTDA, CRESDA, all registration-only) or 2014 TLEs plus the operator's pointing record.

+### Q2.2 Method for an exact overpass and footprint (to run once Space-Track access exists)
+1. Download `gp_history` TLEs for each NORAD ID with epochs from 14 to 28 March 2014: `https://www.space-track.org/basicspacedata/query/class/gp_history/NORAD_CAT_ID/<id>/EPOCH/2014-03-14--2014-03-28/orderby/EPOCH/format/tle`.
+2. Choose the TLE whose epoch is nearest each image date. Propagate with `sgp4`/`skyfield` at 1 s steps over 00:00–08:00 UTC.
+3. For each descending pass, find the time of closest approach to the target and the off-nadir/incidence angle. Keep the passes whose incidence angle lies within the sensor's agility limits.
+4. Where an incidence angle is published (THEOS 26.982451°), select the pass that reproduces it. Where a time is published (04:16Z, 03:07:56Z), check that it matches.
+5. Footprints are the swath polygon from step 3 (strip length unknown). Catalogue metadata are the only route to exact corners.
+
+### Q2.3 Nominal pass times (computed here; circular orbit, node local time held fixed, mean solar time)
+Assumption: on a descending pass going south, the local mean time of the nadir point at latitude φ is LTDN − atan(|cos i|·tan x)/15 h, where sin x = sin|φ|/sin i. The UTC of a nadir pass over the target longitude λ is that local time minus λ/15. Each 100 km of cross-track offset shifts the time by about 5 min at 44–46° S.

+## Q3. Who tasked and analysed imagery; area imaged; evidence of examination; non-detection maps
+
+### Q3 short answer
+**Tasking.** AMSA, working with the Australian Geospatial-Intelligence Organisation (AGO, formerly DIGO), asked foreign governments to image the search area around 15 March 2014. The ATSB lists France, Italy, Germany, Thailand, China and the United States, and says imaging was requested in radar, optical and infrared [S7 PDF pp.125–126 / pp.116–117]. Malaysia separately asked "the US, China and France amongst others" for satellite data on 16 March [S4].
+
+**Analysis.**
+- AGO analysed commercial DigitalGlobe imagery "frame by frame" and passed the 16 March detections to AMSA on 20 March [S1 Update 7; P9].
+- China's SASTIND analysed Gaofen-1 imagery.
+- Malaysia's MRSA analysed the Airbus imagery and found 122 objects in about 400 km² [S5].
+- GISTDA processed THEOS imagery over two days [P7].
+- Japan's Cabinet Satellite Intelligence Center analysed IGS imagery [P8].
+- The French Ministry of Defence made the first analysis of its Pléiades images and found 4 possible objects (ATSB [S7 PDF p.127]; GA says two of the French detections were re-detected [S8 p.6]).
+
+**Total area imaged: NOT FOUND.** No agency published a total. The only per-image areas found are about 400 km² (MRSA), >450 km² (GISTDA; the director also said "about 175 square miles" [P6]), and 4 × about 500 km² (GA scenes). AMSA said it re-tasked satellites to the new area on 28 March [S1 Update 23/24].
+
+**Footprint or non-detection maps: none found.** No map or list of imaged footprints with "nothing detected" was published in the ATSB report, the AMSA releases or timeline, the JACC releases, the Malaysian MOT statements or the GA Record. Imagery therefore **cannot** currently serve as a non-detection observation. The nearest usable negative evidence is the *aerial* search of the satellite-cued areas: 22,869–74,420 km² per day, 20–27 March [S7 Table 12, PDF p.33 / p.24].
+
+**What the ATSB said about the imagery programme.**
+- Seven of the satellite detections between 16 and 27 March were in 43–45° S, 90–97° E, with objects 5–24 m long. That area "was extensively searched by aircraft between 20 and 27 March 2014 without locating any MH370 related debris" [S7 PDF pp.32, 126].
+- Radar and optical detections on 20–23 March at 34–35.5° S, 90–92° E were never searched by air; two merchant ships transiting the area reported nothing [S7 PDF p.126].
+
+**What GA said (Minchin et al. 2017).** GA was engaged only in March 2017. It received the four PLEIADES 1A images on 23 March 2017. Its team surveyed more than 6,500 grid cells per image manually, after semi-automatic classification failed because the objects were spectrally like glint. It rated 12 of 70 objects "probably man-made". It "cannot determine whether they are aircraft debris". It recommended examining comparable images where no debris is expected, to calibrate the false-alarm rate [S8 pp.5–9, 24].

+## Q4. Investigators' conclusions per sighting
+
+### Q4 short answer
+Every southern-area sighting (16, 18, 23-MRSA, 24 and 26 March) was followed up by aircraft and/or ships, and none was relocated or linked to MH370:
+- **16 Mar:** AMSA re-oriented four aircraft to a 23,000 km² area [S1 Update 7].
+- **18 Mar:** AMSA plotted the Chinese object inside the 22 March search area; "The object was not sighted" [S2].
+- **23 Mar (MRSA):** the positions "were within Wednesday's [26 March] search area" [S1 Update 20].
+- **24 and 26 Mar:** the Thai and Japanese positions arrived on 27–28 March. By then the search had moved 1,100 km north-east on 28 March, because of revised radar and fuel analysis [S1 Updates 23/24; S4 28 Mar].
+
+ATSB: the 43–45° S area was searched 20–27 March "without locating any MH370 related debris" [S7 PDF p.126]. JACC (A. Houston, 28 April 2014): "we haven't found anything anywhere that has any connection to MH370, and that includes the satellite imagery" [S10]. ATSB (M. Dolan, July 2014, as quoted secondhand **[independent]** [I5]): the search "has not found any debris associated with the satellite images".
+
+The northern detections (20–23 March, 34–35.5° S) were never searched by air [S7 PDF p.126]. They were revisited only in 2017 via the Pléiades re-analysis. ATSB described the result as possible debris that "cannot be concluded with certainty" to be MH370 debris, but whose coincidence with the other analyses is "compelling" [S7 PDF p.123]. CSIRO back-tracked the objects to a most likely impact point of 35.6° S 92.8° E [S9 §4; S7 PDF p.122].

+   - A second detection at **07:23:00 UTC, S37.8 E92.93**.
+   - Images: `https://370Location.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/thermal-plume-044630-500m-normalized.png`, `…/thermal-plume-072300-500m-normalized.png`.
+   - A Meteosat-7 stack: `…/2016/11/Meteosat7-RGBWVIRnull-8mar0000-8mar0800-frame30min-stack-crop-640x480-crf5.webm`.
+
+   The page labels the 04:46 pass "Aqua" and the 07:23 pass "Terra". The CMR granule list below shows a **Terra** day granule at 04:45–04:50 UTC over the area, and no Terra granule near 07:23. The labels therefore appear swapped (an inference from orbit timing).
+4. **Kristensen (arXiv:1811.09315, v4 2024) [independent, not peer-reviewed]** [I7]. This preprint claims contrail segments near Christmas Island in two consecutive Meteosat-7 frames (Weather Graphics source), ending with a turn at 00:08.

+### Q5(b) Critiques
+- **Trailspotter (Metabunk, 18–20 April 2015)** [C1]:
+  - contrails drift with wind, so an aged contrail can lie "tens of miles away" from the flight path;
+  - forecast relative humidity was about 45% at the turn location and low on the 31–38° S line, unfavourable for long contrails;
+  - the 31–38° S dark line did not move relative to the cloud deck over an hour, whereas a shadow should move with sun angle and wind, so it "is almost certainly a natural gap in the cloud layer";
+  - the line is too long and too wide for a distrail, and geostationary resolution is far coarser than MODIS 250 m.
+- Another Metabunk user (Herman Aven) noted the **selection bias**: the route was known before the search for lines, and no hit/miss rate was given [C1].
+- **Vasquez's own caveats** [I2]: 2 km resolution at a steep slant angle, low albedo at dawn, possible noise or a cirrus back edge. His feature at 46.2° S 87.3° E was tied to a hypothesised 210° magnetic heading.
+- **370location's own Feb 2017 update** [I4]: the "plume" formed from pre-existing water vapour before 00:00 UTC, which "appears to rule out" an impact plume.
+- **ATSB/NASA (official)** [S7 PDF pp.126–127]: NASA (Minnis et al.) examined polar-orbiter imagery from 19:00 UTC on 7 March to 05:00 UTC on 8 March. They found one candidate, Terra MODIS **04:45 UTC, 45.5° S 84° E**, and concluded it was "probably a shadow from some other feature or a cloud edge". A Sumatra candidate was judged a cloud-deck discontinuity.
+
+**Prior project conclusion:** no record found in project memory, this session's archive, or the Drive checkpoint search. The only Drive hit was a 2024 blog-comment page. Pete's recollection (cloud, not contrail) is **consistent with** the official NASA/ATSB finding and with the Metabunk and 370location self-corrections, but it was not located as a project document.

+### Q5(c) Public raw data for re-analysis
+
+**Meteosat-7 IODC.** The satellite provided IODC from 57° E between 05 Dec 2006 and 31 Mar 2017 [D1]. It is archived in the EUMETSAT Data Store as **EO:EUM:DAT:0881 "MVIRI Level 1.5 Climate Data Record Release 2 – MFG – 57 degree"** [D2], with free registration. The MVIRI ground pixel is 2.5 km at the sub-satellite point [D3] (nominal IR about 5 km; VIS half-pixels).
+
+**MODIS (Terra/Aqua) and VIIRS (Suomi NPP).** Granule search is public via NASA CMR, without login. Download needs a free Earthdata login (credential available in this project). The search was run for 7 Mar 16:00 to 8 Mar 06:00 UTC over 0–45° S, 80–110° E [D4]. Granule start times (5-minute MODIS, 6-minute VIIRS):
