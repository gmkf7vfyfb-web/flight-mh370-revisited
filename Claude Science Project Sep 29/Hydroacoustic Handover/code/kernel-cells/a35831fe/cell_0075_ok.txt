+## 2. Basics and first principles (ASD-STE100)
+
+### 2.1 The chain
+
+An impact makes sound. The sound goes through the ocean to a hydrophone. The hydrophone records it with noise. We can see the sound only if it is louder than the noise.
+
+The received sound energy is:
+
+received energy = impact energy × coupling × spectrum share ÷ transmission loss
+
+- **Impact energy (E).** This is the kinetic energy at water contact. End of flight gives it for each simulated impact.
+- **Coupling (η).** This is the fraction of E that becomes sound in the water. No theory gives it for a large aircraft. We must get it from real crashes.
+- **Spectrum share.** This is the part of the sound in the frequency band that we measure (5–40 Hz). It depends on the impact duration and on the source depth.
+- **Transmission loss (TL).** This is the loss along the path. The module calculates it from bathymetry and sound speed. It is checked against airgun shots to about 8 dB r.m.s.
+
+Then three tests apply to each possible signal:
+1. **Time.** Sound in the deep channel travels at 1.482 km/s ± 0.4 %. Over 1,500–3,500 km the arrival time is known to about 6–14 s. This test is strong.
+2. **Place.** One site gives only a time, so it gives a set of possible places. Two or more sites, or a bearing at Cape Leeuwin, give a place. The place must be on the 7th arc and inside the impact prior.
+3. **Loudness.** The recorded level must be possible with a feasible E and a feasible η. This test is weak, because η and TL are uncertain by one to two orders of magnitude.
+
+A signal that passes all three tests is feasible. Feasible does not mean found. It means "not ruled out".
+

+
+### 2.3 Feasible range of coupling (theory and prior examples)
+
+**Theory.** No validated theory gives η for a large aircraft. The literature review (8 Oct) found no measurement of the sound efficiency of a large aircraft water impact. Small-body water-entry theory and explosion theory (the Arons relation) exist. No scaling from them to a 200 t airframe has been validated. The only hard physical limit is η < 1, because sound cannot carry more energy than the impact had.
+
+**Prior examples.** Only one aircraft crash has a measured long-range sound record that we can use: the F-35A off Japan (9 April 2019), recorded at Wake Island (Metz, Obana & Fukao 2023).
+
+| F-35A result (module's own chain, today) | value |
+|---|---|
+| coupling anchor, 10 m source depth | **2.26e-3** |
+| H11N / H11S separately | 3.3e-3 / 1.5e-3 |
+| observed-band lower bound (model-free shape) | 1.2e-3 |
+| if the source depth is 2 m / 30 m | 5.4e-2 / 3.4e-4 |
+| if the impact duration is 0.05 s | 2.8e-4 |
+| if E is 700 / 1,100 MJ | 2.9e-3 / 1.8e-3 |
+| old value used in every overnight number | 2.1e-4 (a different quantity, from Brown 2026) |
+
+Two facts limit this anchor. First, the 900 MJ impact energy comes from Brown (2026), not from Metz. Second, a 19 t fighter in a near-vertical dive at about 300 m/s is not a 200 t airliner. The transfer is not tested (gap G-F4).
+
+Known shallow explosions give a second view. Brown (2026) Table A.4 shows that shallow-source coupling into the deep channel varies over about 3.5 decades between sites.
+
+**Rule used in this review (proposed; it needs your ruling).** The loudness test compares the coupling a signal needs with the F-35A value:
+- up to 10 × the F-35A value: **feasible**. One decade covers the measured F-35A spreads (triads, energy, impact duration, propagation model) and the propagation error at MH370 ranges.
+- 10 to 100 ×: **strained**. This needs coupling well outside the only aircraft example.
+- above 100 ×: **not feasible**. This needs more than about a quarter of the impact energy to become sound.
+- η > 1: **impossible**.

+## 5. Check of the work and results: findings
+
+Each finding states the evidence.
+
+1. **Airgun shots were ranked as candidates.** The module holds the times of 110 airgun shots in the Diego Garcia panels (`h08s_template_shots.csv`). 44 of 47 Diego Garcia candidates lie within 1.5 s of a shot; chance gives 30 %. Every top-5 pair and the "strongest pair" use one of them. The 01:12:49 candidate is 0.14 s from a shot. The shot suppression (−5 to −8 dB) was measured, but the candidates were not gated.
+2. **Known events were not removed.** Royer located 20 events that night, with picks at Cape Leeuwin. Kadri's 00:52:03 lies inside Royer's pick bar for icequake A on his Fig. 4 (00:51:56.8–00:52:04.5). Kadri's 00:54:30 is 6 s from Royer's arrow for event 5. Kadri's poster box 4 contains Royer's pick bar for event B. At the IMOS loggers, Royer events 6 and 9 predict arrivals at Perth Canyon 3315 at 01:16:53 and 01:04:29; the module has candidates at 01:16:55 and 01:04:29 (chance of each match about 0.05). The thread knew of the icequake A match at about 10:00 today, but the pair stayed on top of the charts.
+3. **The coupling anchor was 10.3 dB low.** 2.1e-4 (Brown's Arons-equivalent coupling) was used in place of the module's own definition. Metz gives 2.26e-3. Every "× F-35A" number from last night is too high by a factor of 10.8. The ranked tables, the energy chart and the Kadri package still use the old value.
+4. **"330 × the F-35A" was the wrong figure for the question.** That number is the detection threshold of the pair (old anchor). The loudness that the strongest pair needs comes from its Diego Garcia half (an airgun pulse) and is about 380 × the new anchor even at 12 GJ. The Cape Leeuwin half alone needs only about 1 × the F-35A value at 12 GJ. So the Cape Leeuwin signal is loud enough to be an impact. It fails because it is a known icequake, not because of energy.
+5. **Detection power is overstated.** The matched filter was tested only on signals made from its own templates. On the real F-35A arrival it needs +4.0 to +9.5 dB more at Perth Canyon and +6.6 to +11.1 dB more on the Cape Leeuwin substitute noise. Every P_D, η₅₀ and upper limit built on it is optimistic by that amount.
+6. **Scott Reef was used although it carries no information.** It is in 63 singles, 2,584 pairs (78 %) and 11,067 triples (90 %). Its loudness test cannot be computed (blocked path).
+7. **Bands below the filters were searched.** 35 singles and 1,111 pairs use a 2–5 Hz band. The IMOS loggers roll off below 8 Hz. Kadri's processing high-passes at 5 Hz. A 2–5 Hz detection on these records cannot be the impact's 2–5 Hz sound.
+8. **The Cape Leeuwin and Diego Garcia p-values are not calibrated.** The digitised panels are plots at about 14.5 points per second. Their noise reference was an emulated IMOS 3376 plot (a substitute). H01W gave 0.11 of the chance count, which shows the mismatch.
+9. **Overall counts below chance.** 184 / 60 / 29 rows at the 0.3 / 0.1 / 0.05 levels against 236 / 79 / 39 by chance. The noise references are noisier than the night itself. So p-values are conservative and "no detection" carries less weight than stated.
+10. **Substitutes were used without approval.** Paired-logger backgrounds at 3315 and 3275; shaped IMOS noise at H01W and H08S; a reference impact (37.1 S, 1.13 GJ) for single-site coupling, not the implied place; τ and energy stand-ins for supplement rows. Each was declared, but under your rule each needed approval.
+11. **The source depth was fixed at 10 m.** At the F-35A, 2 m or 30 m moves η by more than two decades. The MH370 chain did not run the source-depth sensitivity (G-L4).
+12. **Single-site "implied places" are not places.** At an IMOS logger, a time fits a whole set of points along the arc. The singles table shows one of them.
+13. **One coincidence crosses land.** Kadri's 00:40:34 row (bearing 303°, arc point 26.34 S 100.93 E) predicts Portland 3275 at 01:07:37, and the module has its third-ranked single there (01:07:46, p 0.009). But the direct path from that point to Portland crosses south-west Western Australia (GEBCO, 201 of 2,000 path points above sea level). So that match is not physical by the direct path.
+14. **Searches covered only the pre-registered windows.** Held IMOS records outside them were not searched (for example the 01:30 records that contain the CMST event). This is correct for the MH370 test, but it removed free known-event checks.
+15. **Compute was not the cost.** The heaviest overnight steps took minutes (injection-recovery 25 min, weak-signal 12 min, ranking 12 min, propagation grid 48–75 min, composition 86 min). Only the RAM grid was heavy (31.8 CPU-hours). The 42 % weekly use came from running 11 agent conversations in parallel, not from computation.

+- Royer predictions: geodesic distance ÷ the median path speed from Royer's own picks for that event. Cape Leeuwin uses Royer's own picks.
+- The feasibility tiers (10 × and 100 × the F-35A value) are a proposed rule for your ruling.
+- This review did not re-run any detector, propagation model or likelihood. It did not look at raw IMOS waveforms. It is exploratory and was not pre-registered.
