WHAT IT MEASURES
The SATCOM logs carry received power for the R1200 bursts. A path predicts a received-power departure from:
- the aircraft's attitude: heading, pitch and roll;
- the satellite's direction;
- the installed antenna's gain pattern.
A likelihood compares that prediction with the logs. This is directional information that BTO and BFO don't carry.

PRIOR FINDINGS TO CARRY FORWARD (from the recreation's README; don't re-derive them)
- The held-out comparison between the two precompensation endpoints was inconclusive:
  - full target-EIRP precompensation versus no precompensation: mean log-density difference +0.116 per point, 95% interval [−0.194, +0.462], sign-flip p = 0.536;
  - a forward-only check reversed the sign.
  So the two endpoints stay separate conditional alternatives, with no prior weight between them.
- The event-level s.d. was 1.72 dB, from the blocked no-precompensation RMSE. It wasn't tuned to MH371 truth.
- The gain surface is an uncommissioned first pass:
  - it isn't verified as the installed pattern;
  - it has no port/starboard handoff behaviour;
  - it disagrees materially with the workbook gain at the first MH371 event.
- Call-channel (C-channel) received power was not integrated, for three reasons: no calibration, a free offset absorbs the signal, and serial dependence within a burst.
- Use at the end of flight also needs HPA output limits, antenna side selection and restart-channel power calibration.
- A mistake not to repeat: in the old work, MH371 was not run blind (the window moved after the truth was seen). Here, only the core's MH371 scorer reads the ACARS truth.
