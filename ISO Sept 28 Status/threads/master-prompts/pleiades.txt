YOUR JOB: "Pleiades" (hypothesis work: hypotheses/pleiades/, created with `make new-hypothesis H=pleiades`)
A conditional hypothesis. H: at least one object imaged by Pleiades on 23 March 2014 (Geoscience Australia Record 2017/13) came from 9M-MRO.
- The module returns the log-likelihood ratio of the object data under H versus not-H, given the impact location.
- The prior π on H is swept, not fixed. The composer then reports p(x | D, H), P(H | D) and the mixture.
- Results are explicit conditional alternatives, never the base estimate.
- The drift physics comes from the shared ocean-transport component (crates/ocean, built by the "Ocean drift" thread); coordinate with them. Until it exists, work on:
  - the object table and clustering;
  - the background density;
  - the likelihood design and tests;
  - a comparison with Griffin & Oke's method.

DESIGN GUIDANCE
- log L = ln[(1−π) + π Σ_c w_c p(y_c|s)/q_c], summed over object clusters. The 12 rating-5 objects are really about 4 locations: PHR_4 north, PHR_4 south, PHR_3, PHR_1.
- Use an analytic spread (random walk + transport-model error calibrated on drifters) so p(y|s) is normalised and seed-free.
- q_c is a documented background density over the imaged footprint.
- Avoid counting Stokes drift twice: Griffin & Oke's 1.2% windage is a Stokes proxy, so don't add WAVERYS Stokes on top.
- The ocean model is the shared alternative "ocean-model".
- Open questions:
  - scene footprints, and a Poisson term for debris that should have been seen but wasn't;
  - whether to include the rating-4 objects (PHR_2 object 12 has lat and lon swapped in GA's table);
  - a correlated multi-object likelihood;
  - the size of the 15-day transport error.
