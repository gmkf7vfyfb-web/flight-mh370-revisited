THE ARCHITECTURE YOU PLUG INTO
The thread "Core stages & composer" is building this now, on branch core/stages.

One estimator, run as four stages:
1. The core filter runs from the 18:01 prior (or an 18:22 prior) through the SATCOM data. For the integrated estimate it stops at 00:11.
2. An end-of-flight stage continues each posterior trajectory:
   - powered flight on the core dynamics until the end-of-flight module takes over;
   - the module simulates descents to impact;
   - the runner scores whichever 00:19 messages the config selects.
3. Impact-level modules each return a log-likelihood per impact sample.
4. A composer combines any chosen set of modules into one posterior. It reports evidence, effective sample size (ESS) and replicate agreement, and writes summary.json for the report.

Hooks:
- The existing four stay: adjust_prior, extra_epochs, epoch_log_likelihood, final_log_likelihood.
- Phase 1 adds:
  - alternatives(): discrete alternatives with prior probabilities.
    - Trajectory-level alternatives run as separate strata, like autopilot modes.
    - Impact-level alternatives return one log-likelihood per alternative.
    - An alternative shared across modules (e.g. "ocean-model") is declared by name and kept aligned.
  - impact_log_likelihood(impact, alternative).
  - Named predictions per impact sample, for predictive-only modules.
  - A terminal (end-of-flight) hook.
  - A list of the observation IDs each module consumes.
- The exact Rust signatures will come from thr_4pbhvf3sxi. Design your science so it drops into these hooks.

Impact sample fields (provisional): time, latitude, longitude, velocity (east, north, up), flight-path angle, mass, kinetic energy (total and vertical), descent family, parent trajectory, weight.

Composition rules every module follows:
1. Return the likelihood of your data given the state, never a posterior. Remove any internal reference prior yourself. Say whether your likelihood is on an absolute scale; only absolute-scale likelihoods can be mixed across alternatives.
2. Each observation is used once per run. List the observation IDs you consume.
3. All impact modules use the same impact samples. Never smooth privately over impact location: the product of separately smoothed likelihoods is not the smoothed product.
4. Likelihoods must be smooth, with explicit model error. No floors. "Not computed" is not "impossible". Cover the prior's support or refuse.
5. Be accurate where the other evidence is: where the flight posterior has its mass, roughly 30–40°S along the 7th arc, not only near your own peak.
6. Measure Monte Carlo adequacy (ESS, split-half replicate agreement). Report unconverged results as unconverged; the fix is the sampler, never smoothing.
7. Settle discrete model choices by evidence, or show them as labelled sensitivities. Never use fixed-weight averages of normalised maps.
8. Include one synthetic-recovery test (generate your data from a known impact and check coverage), plus hand-computed fixtures.

CALIBRATION STATUS (checked 25 Sep 2026)
Propagation can be partly calibrated.
- The 2001 airgun site air9 (27°33.6′S 98°52.6′E, 19 Oct 2001, 02:10–02:46 UTC) is about 116 km from the 7th arc. It was recorded at both H01 (1,665 km) and H08S.
  - Source: Blackman et al., SIO/LLNL report UCRL-TR-207323, Table 1, Fig. 4 and Fig. 23.
  - Measured transmission loss: about 120–133 dB at H01 (10–60 Hz) and 122–135 dB at H08S (5–60 Hz).
  - Site air8 was not detected at H01, and nobody could explain why.
- SUS charge site A11 (13.2°S 104.7°E, 2003).
- Kadri 2024 does not cite this work; Metz et al. 2023 does.

The source term cannot be calibrated from public information.
- The only aircraft data point is the F-35A recorded at H11 (Brown et al. 2026, Icarus): 900±200 MJ, 0.7 Pa at 3,300 km, η of order 1e-4.
  - It is an upper bound, because the crash site had a shallow sound channel.
- A 2023 modelling abstract (Duncan & Dall'Osto) puts MH370's path to HA01 20–30 dB worse. Predicted HA01 levels would then be at or below the noise in Kadri's traces.

DETECTION-CONTROL PROTOCOL (agreed with Pete; implement it, and hand it to Kadri)
(a) Pre-register the search before looking:
- windows per station from the impact posterior (arrival-time and bearing ranges);
- bands, e.g. 2–5, 5–10, 10–20, 20–40 Hz;
- the detector and its threshold.
(b) Build backgrounds from real data:
- the same station outside the window. ±60 min is a start; ±6 h gives about 20 windows;
- the same UTC window on other nights, e.g. 1–14 March 2014;
- off-bearing windows;
- time slides between stations, for coincidences.
(c) Characterise the noise per station and hydrophone:
- band power percentiles (5/50/95);
- burstiness;
- flagged source classes: seismic airguns (the TGS Huzzas survey at H08S), earthquake T-phases checked against catalogues, blue and fin whale calls at 15–30 Hz, ships;
- the detector's false-alarm rate against threshold.
(d) Inject synthetic impact signals into real background data and recover them. This gives detection probability against received level, and the timing and bearing errors.
(e) Positive controls: the seismic survey, catalogued earthquakes, and the ~01:34 UTC HA01 event that ATSB called likely geological.
(f) Combining near the noise floor:
- coherent triad beamforming at one station, about 5 dB of gain;
- cross-station association by consistent times and bearings;
- significance from the time slides.
(g) The likelihood form, once calibrated: (1 − P_D) + P_D × (match to detections ÷ background rate). Pete's rule: an absence counts only where a signal should have been detectable.
